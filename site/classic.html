Quick tour

Sinking ground around the world

Europe and North America already have free ground-motion maps. Most of the fastest-sinking places don't. Groundline uses NASA-ISRO's NISAR radar to measure them, and says plainly where it can't.

04 Why it matters here

  • Subsidence has cut the Friant-Kern Canal's capacity by more than 60% along a 33-mile reach, from about Pixley to the Kern County line. SJV Water
  • Design capacity 4,000 cubic feet per second versus about 1,600. U.S. Bureau of Reclamation
  • A 10-mile Phase 1 fix cost $326 million, as reported. SJV Water
  • The State Water Board may vote in April 2027 on a proposed pumping moratorium within two miles of the canal in southern Tulare County. It is a proposal and has not been adopted. SJV Water
  • State Water Board staff, 2024: the quarterly monitoring provided by InSAR data may not be frequent enough to avoid exceedances. Staff report, p. 105 (PDF)
  • California's Department of Water Resources publishes Sentinel-1 InSAR subsidence maps at 100 m, quarterly. data.gov

This app adds an independent, free map from NISAR radar, checked against GPS stations that were left out of the fit, so anyone can see how far to trust it.

    06 How it works

    Six steps, all from public NISAR data (reading the full products needs a free Earthdata login). San Joaquin Valley still uses the team's earlier research export, built from preview images. The code is in pipeline/.

    Show the six steps

    1. Find

      Ask NASA's Alaska Satellite Facility catalogue for every NISAR interferogram over the place.

    2. Read

      Read just the area we need from each full radar product, with its ionosphere correction and signal-quality layers.

    3. Clean

      Remove the ionosphere effect, mask places where the radar signal is unreliable, and take out leftover tilts and weather effects that follow the terrain.

    4. Stack

      Chain the 12-day pairs into a time series, with an uncertainty for every cell.

    5. Check

      Compare with continuous GPS stations and, where it exists, NASA's OPERA ground-motion product.

    6. Publish

      Write dated map layers and a small data file that this page reads.

    07 What this can and cannot tell you

    ✓ It can

    • Show where the ground moved by more than the noise level between radar passes, and roughly by how much.
    • Rank stretches of a canal, road or rail line by how much they moved, with an uncertainty for each.
    • Say exactly which radar passes each map is built from, and how they compare with other published data.
    • Be checked: every curve can be put next to an independent GPS station.

    ✗ It cannot, or not yet

    • Line of sight, not true vertical. The radar measures along its slanted view. Vertical is roughly line of sight divided by 0.77, with an assumed viewing angle.
    • Simple corrections, coarse cells. The products' own ionosphere correction is applied and unreliable cells are masked, but no further weather model is used, and cells are about 240 to 480 m. San Joaquin still comes from an earlier export built from preview pictures, without these corrections.
    • It over-reads at slowly moving stations.
    • It disagrees in size with the state product. For overlapping months the NISAR amplitude is about 1.6 times California's published product. We have not resolved why.
    • It fails where motion is slow or the air is humid. We tried Houston and Jakarta: not detected.
    • Short record. Data for many regions begins in mid-June 2026.
    • Not for design or enforcement. Use it to decide where to look and whom to call, then confirm with surveyed data and official products.
